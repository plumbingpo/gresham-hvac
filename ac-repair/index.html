When the air conditioning quits in Gresham, OR, the house tells you fast, and so does everyone in it. The most common failure we see is the humble run capacitor: a few dollars of electronics that starts the compressor and fan, weakens quietly for years, and finally lets go on a brutal afternoon, leaving the outdoor unit humming but the fan still. Right behind it come pitted contactors that no longer pass power cleanly, condenser fan motors with dry bearings, refrigerant leaks that slowly bleed the system's capacity away, and evaporator coils that ice over solid whenever airflow collapses behind a clogged filter.

At Gresham HVAC, each cooling call starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because a single warm-air symptom can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

Warning Signs for AC Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Water dripping from the indoor unit or showing up on the ceiling below
  • Breaker trips or a burning odor at startup
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • Rising electric costs paired with a sticky, uncomfortable house
  • A hiss or gurgle from the lines, a common sign of leaking refrigerant
  • Vents blowing air that is barely cooler than the room
  • The outdoor unit humming or clicking but the fan not spinning

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

Consider the refrigerant first. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money leaks out with the refrigerant.

What you get from a pro is a repair that holds. Our experienced technicians find leaks with dye and electronic detectors, base every charge decision on superheat and subcooling, test electrical parts with a meter before condemning them, and measure static pressure and inspect the blower and filter whenever airflow is in question. Parts-cannon fixes tend to bring you back in a few weeks. Ours are built to last through the heat. And if the numbers say replacement is the better call, we show you those numbers honestly, without pressure.

Maintenance Tips for AC Repair

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Schedule a spring tune-up so weak parts are caught before summer arrives
  • Never enclose the condenser; it needs open space on every side to shed heat
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Pay attention to buzzing or clicking when the AC starts, a common warning of a failing capacitor
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
