A heat pump is an air conditioner that learned to run in reverse: in summer it moves heat out of your house, and in winter a reversing valve flips the refrigerant flow so the same compressor pulls heat from outdoor air and delivers it inside. Because it moves heat rather than generating it, its efficiency runs far past anything resistance heating can do, which is why so many Gresham, OR homeowners are switching as older equipment ages out. Modern cold-climate models with variable-speed inverter compressors keep producing useful heat far below freezing, a genuine engineering leap over the units that gave the technology its old reputation.

Getting that performance takes design work up front, and this is where Gresham HVAC earns the job. Sizing a heat pump means reconciling two loads: the summer cooling demand and the winter heating demand, and finding the balance point where outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, weigh the compressor staging options against your climate and ducts, and design the backup heat correctly, whether that is electric strips staged to assist below the balance point or a dual-fuel pairing with a gas furnace that takes over in the deepest cold. We also verify the electrical service and the duct system, because heat pumps deliver their heat at lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need real airflow to feel right.

On installation day we follow the same standards used on every Gresham HVAC system. The line set is pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, the charge is weighed in and checked with superheat and subcooling, and the outdoor unit goes on a stand wherever winter defrost drainage requires it. Condensate is handled properly, and the thermostat is configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost all work as designed. The Benefits of Professional Heat Pump Installation

Heat pumps punish installation shortcuts more than any other residential system because they work both sides of the year. A charge that is slightly off hurts twice, in cooling efficiency all summer and heating capacity all winter. A balance point never calculated means electric backup strips quietly carrying the house at several times the operating cost while the homeowner wonders why the bills did not drop. A thermostat left on defaults can run backup heat when the compressor alone would do, or fight the defrost cycle. And an outdoor unit set flat on the ground in a real winter sits in its own refrozen defrost water until the coil is a block of ice.

We design and install to prevent every one of those problems. Loads are calculated for heating and cooling, equipment is selected for its actual capacity at your winter design temperature, backup heat is staged and locked out properly, the outdoor unit is raised for defrost drainage, and the charge is weighed and verified instead of guessed. Commissioning covers both modes, including a check of the reversing valve and a full defrost cycle. Refrigerant handling is federally regulated, and inverter equipment brings electronics that reward attention to detail. Installed correctly, a heat pump gives your home the quietest, steadiest comfort available.

How to Prevent Heat Pump Installation Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Give the outdoor unit open space all year, free of drifts and overgrowth
  • Light frost and an occasional defrost hiss are normal in winter, but solid ice calls for service
  • Keep an eye on winter bills; a sharp increase usually means the backup heat is running too much
  • Keep temperature settings steady so the backup strips are not needed to catch up
  • Clean the outdoor coil each spring for a strong start to summer
  • Replace filters on time, since restricted airflow hurts heat pump performance year-round
  • Schedule spring and fall maintenance to keep the charge and defrost checked
